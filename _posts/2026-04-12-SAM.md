SAM is the world's first portable autonomous food robot. By fitting inside of a standard 20ft ISO shipping container, SAM can be integrated into existing logistics, bringing cooked to order meals to people no matter the location.

![SAM-on-truck]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/SAM_on_truck.jpg)

I was the lead designer of SAM and was involved with the project from the very beginning, from the initial concept to the final design and implementation. It was an incredible experience to work on such a groundbreaking project and to see it come to life.

Here I will share what I did on the project, using publicly available information.

## My Role in SAM
During the project I wore many hats - from leading the design to debugging code to getting coffee for my coworkers. Here I want to highlight three of the important roles:

1. Strategic Designer - defining and scoping the goals of SAM for the goodBytz and our clients.
2. Design Team Lead - leading the small but mighty 3 people design team at goodBytz.
3. Individual Contributor - some extra bits and bops that I did.

### As Strategic Designer
At the earliest stage of the project my role was to navigate the strategic layer of the design of SAM: what is the food robot our client wants and how close to that ideal can our engineering team (and the constraints of the timeline) get. I ran multiple sessions with stakeholders from product, sales, and engineering to help scope the ambitions for SAM, maintaining a focus on deployability (rather than portability) while retaining the experience goodBytz has built up over the years with made-to-order cooking.

![SAM-pre-production]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/SAM_pre_production.jpg)
I am particularly proud of my work towards coming up with the idea of preparing 30 meals in advance, enabling a high dish serving rate during peak times while maintaining food quality and reducing deployment times. In addition to the ideation, I also helped pitch the idea internally and to clients, check the idea for feasibility from mechanical and culinary perspectives, and developed the mental model for how the system and operators should handle the pre-production.

### As Design Team Lead
I was very happy to be able to lead the 3 person design team that was attached to SAM. Using an interactive, user-centered design process we focused on how to make SAM not only performant but easy to use for two distinct user groups: the operators who clean, load, and run SAM, and the guests who order and enjoy the food.
![SAM-interface]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/SAM_ordering_interface.jpg)

Our outcomes included packout procedures and ergonomic analyses of the operator workflows, and an innovative and aesthetic ordering interface.
